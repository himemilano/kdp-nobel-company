# Chapter 52

The icy air of the vault struck the stripped, skinless surface of Elena’s right palm like a deluge of boiling acid. Blinding white heat spiked up her arm, tearing through her optic nerves until her vision flared silver and crimson. 

She did not draw back. She did not scream.

Bracing her bruised left shoulder against Nicholas’s wet collarbone, she forced her flayed right hand directly into the jagged split of his open chest cavity.

Her raw flesh slammed against the jagged, bone-white edges of his fractured sternum. Beneath the slick layer of pooling blood, her fingers locked around the cold, writhing cable of black and gold coiled tightly around his aorta.

Nicholas’s entire frame convulsed. The violent tremor rattled through his ribs, sending a spray of dark blood over her bare forearm. In the air above them, his right hand—curled into a brutal claw meant to crush his own beating heart—froze inches from his chest. The viridian glare in his left eye flickered violently, struggling against the sudden intrusion of her bare, bleeding hands inside his chest.

 Elena squeezed. She dug her skinless fingertips deeper into his chest cavity, hooking her bare tendons directly behind the parasitic node. Pain radiated from her flayed hand through her ribs, her broken body screaming under the strain, but she locked her teeth until her gums bled.

 Argent filaments erupted straight from the exposed muscle of her open palm. 

They did not manifest as a harmless aura of light. They burst outward like fine, serrated silver wires, threading themselves through her exposed nerve endings and biting directly into the black umbilical tether. The metallic threads hummed, sawing through the parasite's thick, rubbery outer husk.

Instead of drawing the corruption into herself, Elena reversed her breath. She pushed her own blood—dense, hot, and heavy with the ancient Weaver lineage—straight through the argent sutures and into the core of the parasite.

Nicholas threw his head back and let out a sound torn from a blood-soaked throat. His chest heaved as the counter-pulse slammed into his heart. The suffocating viridian sheen vanished from his left eye, replaced instantly by the stark, terrifying brilliance of cold sapphire frost.

"Elena..." his voice was a wet, ragged whisper, choked with blood.

Reclaiming his own body through sheer, monstrous willpower, Nicholas gripped the left side of his cracked ribcage with his bare hand. Muscle tore and tendons groaned as he dragged his own bone structure wide, opening his chest by another inch to give her room. 

"Cut it," he growled, the Alpha authority returning to his voice even as blood welled over his lips. "Do it now."

Elena hooked her argent sutures behind the central root node and pulled with everything she had left. 

A wet, sickening snap echoed through the chamber. The umbilical tether splinters into decaying filaments, spraying black fluid across the stone. The sudden release of tension exploded outward in a shockwave of silver and shadow, throwing both of them back across the slick flagstones.

Nicholas crashed hard onto his side, clutching his bleeding chest as he coughed up thick, dark clumps of blood. The black veins along his neck receded, leaving his skin pale and clammy from massive blood loss. He lay there, his deep chest rising and falling in ragged, shallow gasps that whistled through his cracked sternum.

Elena tumbled into the shadow of the central altar, her flayed hand cradled against her waist. Every nerve end in her right palm felt as though it were being peeled back by hooks, and every shallow breath through her fractured ribs delivered a fresh stab of agony. 

She blinked through a haze of sweat and blood, watching Nicholas. 

The Alpha dragged himself onto his hands and knees. His blood dripped heavily onto the stone, tracing dark circles around his trembling fingers. As he pushed himself up, his eyes locked onto the base of the surrounding altar legs.

There were no carved histories to read. There were only the physical relics of what had transpired here centuries ago.

Embedded into the granite floor were heavy iron neck-collars, sized precisely for female shifters. Beneath them, long silver needles stood fixed in the stone, their hollow tips angled upward to draw fluid directly from a captive's spine into the channel below. 

Nicholas’s trembling hand reached out, his bloody fingers brushing against the cold iron of one collar. The truth sat there in the dark, cold and undeniable: his ancestors had not fought the ancient darkness to protect their people. They had built an underground abattoir, chaining Weaver women to the earth to drain their life force and secure their own rule.

His shoulders shook. The formidable, unyielding Alpha of the Silver Creek Pack let out a low, tortured breath. 

He didn't offer an excuse. He didn't speak of historical necessity or political survival. 

Nicholas dragged his heavy, bleeding body across the stone until he reached her. Weakened, pale, and soaked in his own blood, he sank onto his knees before Elena. Lowering his head until his forehead pressed flat against the bloody stone at her bare feet, he bared the back of his neck in absolute, unreserved submission to her judgment.

"My bloodline built this horror," he rasped, his voice scraping against the stone floor. "My pack lives on stolen life. Strike my head from my shoulders, Elena. Take the retribution my lineage owes you."

Elena stared down at the curve of his neck, her breathing ragged. Her right hand throbbed with excruciating pain, the exposed tendons quivering with every heartbeat. 

"Get up, Nicholas," she said, her voice thin but hard as iron. "We don't have time for your penance."

Ignoring the blinding white flashes in her head, Elena forced herself to stand. She used the edge of the central spire for balance, her flayed hand leaving a thick, smeared palm-print across the stone. 

She dragged herself toward the severed stump of the parasitic cord. 

It hadn't withered completely. The thick base embedded in the bedrock was still pulsating, sending heavy, rhythmic vibrations up through the base of the chamber. Elena pressed her uninjured left hand against the rock. The tremor vibrated through her teeth, her jaw, and down into her collarbone.

This vault wasn't just a tomb. It was the lower intake of a vertical shaft—a massive, hollow chimney carved directly up through the mountain's bedrock.

The siphon was pulling upward. 

The dying remnant of the entity hadn't surrendered; it had redirected the vault's intake back up into the Silver Creek Pack Hall directly above them. It was drawing the collective pack-bond of every living wolf upward into the central Lifestone, preparing to crack the territory's foundation from the top down.

"Nicholas," she called out, her voice straining over the growing hum of the stone. "It’s not over. The intake is pulling upward into your Hall."

Nicholas pushed himself off the floor, his face pale as death, his hand pressed tightly against his ruptured sternum to hold his chest together. He took a heavy step toward her, but his leg muscles buckled from severe blood loss, sending him hard against the spire.

Above them, a low, metallic creak echoed through the vaulted ceiling.

The amber husks hanging from the dark rafters began to sway. In unison, the pale, silver-streaked eyelids of the ancient, dried Weaver husks snapped open. Blind, dead eyes stared down into the darkness, reanimated by the violent upward surge of energy passing through the stone.

Before Nicholas could reach her, a heavy iron portcullis slammed down over the lower entryway with a deafening crash, sealing the exit.

The air pressure inside the vault plummeted. A violent down-draft of cold, black air roared through the vertical shaft. The sudden vacuum hit Elena like a physical wall, yanking her off her feet. 

She slammed against the central conduit. The skinless, bare muscle of her right palm was pinned flat against the pull of the rising vortex. A terrifying, heatless black mark burned its way across her exposed flesh, locking her palm to the mouth of the upward shaft.

"Elena!" Nicholas roared, lunging forward despite the tear in his chest.

He didn't make it. 

From the shadows around the altar, six amber husks dropped from the ceiling, their desiccated, bone-thin arms reaching out. They seized Nicholas by his arms, his waist, and his ruined chest, their hardened fingers digging deep into his open wounds as they dragged him violently backward into the dark corners of the vault.
