# Chapter 55

Late afternoon sun slanted through the high canopy, turning the falling oak leaves to shades of amber and bronze. The air carried the crisp bite of dry pine needles, damp autumn earth, and sweet cedar smoke drifting up from the hearth chimneys in the valley below.

Elena Vance rested her right palm against the gnarled bark of the ancient Sentinel Oak.

The last time she had touched this trunk, rain had washed the grit into her eyes and her own blood had smeared across the tangled roots, cold with the bitter sting of public exile. Today, the dark wood felt solid and warm beneath her fingers. Deep down in the bedrock, where the ancient Loom had once churned with frantic, starving hunger, there was only a settled stillness. The roots drank the mountain water in peace. Across the center of Elena’s palm, a seam of pale silver scar tissue caught the setting sun, smooth as river stone. A few silver strands gleamed at her temples, woven into her dark braid—remnants of the power that had remade her from the bone out, resting easily in her flesh.

Down along the timbered fences of the lower courtyard, the sound of laughter rose on the wind. Someone dropped a heavy iron pan; a chorus of good-natured jeers followed, mingled with the sharp yips of half a dozen young pups chasing dry leaves across the gravel.

Footsteps crunched against dry acorns behind her.

Elena did not draw her hand away from the oak. She knew the steady, unhurried tread. Nicholas stopped three paces back, giving her space on the mossy slope. He wore a plain wool tunic dyed deep forest green and working leather bracers, his broad shoulders relaxed against the mountain breeze. In his hands he held two carved wooden mugs, steam curling from the rims with the aroma of hot spiced cider and roasted barley.

His golden eyes met hers. The poisonous green glare and the frozen, haughty distance were entirely gone, replaced by an open, unshakeable warmth.

Elena turned toward him. She closed the distance between them on her own terms, her boots pressing into the carpet of yellow leaves. When she reached for one of the mugs, her fingertips brushed the rough calluses of his knuckles. Their bond stirred—a single thread of silver humming through the center of her ribs, deep and constant.

"Torin charred the flatbread again," Nicholas said, the faintest smile tugging at the corner of his lips. "He claims the oven draft was cursed by dead kings. Marta threw an alder spoon at his ear and took the peel away from him."

Elena took a sip, letting the spiced heat chase away the mountain chill in her throat. "He burns the crust every autumn feast. The table would feel strange if he didn't."

Nicholas looked down at her hand wrapped around the cedar wood, his gaze resting on the pale line across her palm. He reached out, his bare thumb skimming the mark with deliberate care.

"You don't have to stay by the tree when the wind turns cold," he murmured.

Elena placed her free hand over his wrist, feeling the steady, unbroken thud of his pulse against her fingers. "I come here to remember where the road started. And where I chose to stand."

Nicholas lowered his mug to a flat ledge of dark slate. He stepped back half an inch, then extended his right hand toward her, palm open to the twilight. It was an invitation offered in plain view. No commands. No ancient chains of duty. No blood debt demanding her submission.

Elena placed her mug beside his. Without hesitation, she laid her hand in his open grip.

His fingers closed around hers, warm and firm, holding her with unhurried reverence. The mate bond settled across their shoulders like a heavy woolen blanket, binding them breath to breath, sovereign and unbroken.

Below them on the ridge, oil lanterns flickered to life along the stone paths of the Silver Creek Packhouse. Warm yellow light poured across the courtyard, illuminating the long feast tables, the gathered wolves, and the open gates that no longer kept her out.

The Loom remembered its debt, and the earth had found its peace. Hand in hand, they turned away from the old tree and walked down the hill toward home.
