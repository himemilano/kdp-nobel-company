# Chapter 54

The ceiling did not fall; it detonated downward. 

Ten thousand pounds of fractured marble rushed down like an avalanche of solid grey sky. The displacement of air hit Elena first, slamming into her sternum with the weight of a physical blow and flattening the breath in her lungs. 

She did not reach for a spell. She did not pray to dormant bloodlines. 

Elena snapped her left arm upward, muscle fibers straining as her wrist flexed. Twin strands of platinum-white thread exploded from the split skin above her pulse point, driving into the fractured basalt pillars framing the left wall. The silver lines struck deep, anchoring into the rock like harpoons. 

She wrenched her whole upper body backward. 

The physical torque tore through her left shoulder with a nauseating pop. Pain flared hot and sharp behind her eyes as a rib cracked in her chest, but the tension held. The massive marble slab struck the taut platinum lines mid-fall. The thick silver cords shrieked, fraying under impossible weight, but they held for a fraction of a second—long enough to cant the falling mountain half an inch to the west.

Nicholas acted on pure, animalistic survival. 

Despite the shattered cartilage in his chest and lungs filling with red froth, his right leg drove into the stone. With a guttural, choked snarl, his heavy hip slammed into Elena’s side, throwing both of their fused bodies sideways into the narrow water-drain trench beneath the altar dais a heartbeat before impact.

The stone slab hit the floor. 

The deafening roar echoed through the stone, violently shaking the trench. Basalt dust instantly obliterated all light, filling their noses and throats with suffocating grit. The air vanished, replaced by dense ash that made them both choke violently, coughing up blood against the cramped stone walls. 

They were entombed. The drainage trench was barely three feet high, roofed by the fallen slab and floored by ancient, cracked bedrock.

Nicholas lay pinned beneath her, his chest heaving with shallow, wet rattles. His right arm was clamped around her waist, but his left forearm—shredded to the skeletal structure— lay useless against the rubble.

"Leave... the hand... Elena," Nicholas wheezed. Blood bubbled between his teeth, staining his pale lips black in the shadow. His breath reeked of copper and bile. "Cut... the arm free... before the stone settles."

"Shut up," Elena spat, wiping soot from her eyes with her bloody shoulder. Her left arm hung loose, the dislocated joint throbbing with a sickening rhythm. "I’m not leaving my skin inside your chest to die alone, Nicholas. You carry my blood now—you don't get to quit."

The right palm of her hand remained fused solid to the jet-black resin hardened over his cracked chest. Pulling it away tore at her nerve endings as if her arm were encased in cooling molten lead. 

She raised her left hand, dragging her broken shoulder up with a sharp gasp. With her teeth, she pulled a single, razor-thin strand of platinum light from her bleeding wrist. She looped the line around the base of her right thumb, sliding the edge beneath the fused layer of her own palm, pressed tight against the obsidian-dark crust on his sternum.

She didn't hesitate. She pulled.

The silver line sliced through her own skin with a sickening, wet tear. 

Elena flayed the top dermal layer off her own right palm, leaving a square of skin embedded directly inside the dark resin on Nicholas’s chest. 

Vision flashed blinding white. The agony was a jagged stake driven through her nervous system, so sharp and absolute that her vocal cords locked, squeezing a strangled gasp from her throat. 

Nicholas let out a ragged, violent snarl. His heavy hand flew up, fingers locking around her wrist with enough force to creak the forearm structure beneath her skin as he felt her flesh tear away to stay inside his wound. The thread connecting their aortas thrummed violently, sending a pulse of shared, unadulterated pain burning through both of their heart chambers.

She pulled her hand free. Her right palm was a bloody, skinless square of red muscle, leaking bright platinum light into the pitch-black crawlspace. 

"You're... insane," Nicholas rasped, his eyes flashing with a desperate, wild azure beneath the soot. His hand shifted from her wrist to cup the back of her neck, pulling her down until her forehead rested against his bruised temple. His breath was hot, ragged, and thick with red fluid. "A violent, stubborn... lunatic."

"And you're still breathing," she gritted out through clenched teeth, her body shaking from the loss of blood and shock. "Keep it that way."

Using the dim, bioluminescent platinum sheen spilling from her flayed palm, Elena turned and began to crawl forward into the suffocating gloom of the collapsed drain. She dragged Nicholas behind her by the thick leather strap of his shoulder armor, her knees scraping over sharp basalt splinters. 

The drainage trench sloped deeper into the foundations of the sanctuary. The air grew colder, heavy with the stench of stagnant water, old copper, and decayed marrow.

Ten paces down the incline, Elena’s bloody right hand struck a cold obstruction jutting from the ash-covered floor. 

She froze. 

Riveted directly into the bedrock was a line of heavy, rusted metal neck-collars. 

Elena hovered over them, her flayed palm casting a soft, ghastly light over the ancient metal. The inner rims of the collars were lined with jagged silver spikes, angled downward to pierce the carotid veins of whoever wore them. Below each collar, deep gutters were carved into the stone floor, designed to catch and direct flowing blood toward a dark opening deeper in the earth.

Entwined in the rusted spikes were faded remnants of long-decayed hair—braided strands of pure silver thread, identical to the light that hummed inside Elena’s own veins. 

Stamped into the center of each collar was a heavy seal: a three-fanged wolf head positioned over an inverted sun. 

The mark of the First Alpha of the Crestfall line. 

Beside the collars lay massive, fossilized scales—each one as wide as a war shield, embedded into shattered wolf skulls that had been crushed into the stone floor. 

Elena didn't offer a historical lecture. She didn't recount old myths. 

She reached back, grabbed Nicholas’s left wrist, and forced his large, trembling hand down onto the iron seal of the collar, then shoved his palm against the scale fused to the wolf skull.

Nicholas’s fingers closed over the raised crest of his ancestor. Then his fingertips brushed the silver teeth designed to drain a Weaver's lifeblood, and the fossilized scale of the entity beneath them.

His body went rigid. 

A terrible, ragged rattle escaped his chest. The sapphire light in his right eye dimmed, replaced by a hollow, sickening realization that needed no words. He felt the cold geometry of the metal. He felt the silver spikes. He felt his bloodline's mark stamped directly above the slaughter troughs.

"The first treaty," Nicholas whispered, the words dragging out of his throat like glass. His hand trembled violently against the rusted collar. "It wasn't a defense... My ancestors... didn't fight the serpent."

He looked up at her through the dark, blood running from his nostrils. 

"They built a farm," he rasped, his voice breaking under the weight of his own lineage. "They chained your mothers here... and fed them down the chute... to buy their own power."

Before Elena could answer, the bedrock beneath the collars vibrated.

Deep below the drain, a sound echoed upward through the hollow stone—a slow, wet, subterranean inhalation. 

The dark pitch remaining inside Nicholas’s chest wound reacted instantly to the scent of the fresh blood leaking from Elena’s skinless palm. The obsidian crust liquefied, bubbling like boiling oil, and began sinking inward into his chest cavity.

It caught the platinum thread tethered to his heart.

The dark pitch pulled the thread downward, dragging it past his cracked sternum, pulling it toward his lungs and the spinal column behind it.

Nicholas gasped, his back arching off the stone floor as his chest collapsed inward. His jaw locked open, eyes rolling into his head as his pulse flickered, dropping toward nothingness.

The silver thread yanked taut against Elena’s own heart. The tension dragged her forward, threatening to tear the aorta right out of her ribcage if the line disappeared into his chest.

If she severed the thread, Nicholas would die in seconds. 

If she let the pitch drag it down, the entity beneath the floor would pull her heart through his body.

Elena didn't cut the line. 

With a low, feral snarl, she plunged her skinless, bloody right hand directly into the open, torn gap of Nicholas’s chest wound.

Her bare fingers forced their way past his fractured ribs, slipping through slick, warm blood and dark fluid, until her hand clamped hard around the platinum heart-knot right above his lungs.

The bedrock beneath them shattered. 

The entire floor of the drainage tunnel gave way, dropping both of them straight down into the pitch-black maw of the nest below.
