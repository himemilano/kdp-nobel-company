# Chapter 49

The ash-stitched husk that had once been Rafe took a single step forward across the altar’s slick marble floor. Grey flakes drifted from his cheekbones with every jerk of his throat, revealing the pitch-black loom-threads holding his calcified frame together. His jaw sagged open, dry cinders spilling over calcified lips.

“Give the altar your blood, Alpha,” a voice rasped from the hollow ribcage, layered with a resonant, metallic echo that belonged to no living wolf. “Sustain the lattice, or watch the stone swallow her whole. You cannot hold both sides of the tear.”

Nicholas did not negotiate. His gaze narrowed to shards of bruised iron catching dying forge-fire, hard and unreadable. He knew the illusion of a forced choice when an enemy laid it out. 

With a low, guttural snap of his breath, Nicholas drove his own curved claws straight into the center of his chest.

The tear tore through flesh and muscle with a wet, brutal crunch. Deep red, silver-streaked blood welled over his knuckles, steaming as it met the freezing subterranean air. Before the ash-puppet could react, Nicholas lunged forward and slammed his slick, bleeding palm directly onto the outer wall of the crystal basin.

He was not anchoring the stone. He was forcing a raw blood-circuit into the quartz.

The thick fluid sizzled against the translucent barrier, burning line by line through the blighted outer frost. Light flared where his palm bound to the mineral—a harsh, oxidized silver discharge that struck the crystal matrix like a hammer blow.

***

Inside the heart of the Lifestone, space moved like pressurized ice.

Elena suspended in the dense, light-refracting core, her breath coming in shallow, ragged gasps. Drops of blood escaping her nostrils froze mid-air, suspending tiny red beads in the chartreuse glow surrounding her. The cold pressed inward, a crushing weight against her ribs, seeking to turn her bone marrow to stone.

Then the shockwave hit.

A streak of silver-tinged blood burst through the outer layer of the crystal shell above her. The impact vibrated through her teeth, a sharp, metallic note that shattered the stasis holding her limbs.

Her ancestral Weaver lineage—dormant in the quiet folds of her blood until pushed to the brink of extinction—ignited in response to Nicholas's violent intrusion. 

Elena did not wait for the crystal to crush her. She thrust both hands outward. Ten thin strands of argent light erupted from under her bleeding fingernails, whipping through the pressurized medium like liquid mirror-wire. 

Through the dense quartz, she traced the pitch-black strands that Rafe’s ash-husk was feeding into the base of the Loom. They were thick, oily ropes of shadow, leaching the foundational energy of the altar to feed the Serpent's anchor.

*The pack-bond.* 

Though frayed and bleeding at the edges, the phantom ache where her soul locked into Nicholas’s remained. She focused on that raw, phantom pain, transforming the severing ache into a physical bridge. Wrapping her argent threads around the ghost-resonance of their broken bond, she pulled the silver light taut.

*The Silver Knot.*

She drove her heels into the floor of the inner chamber, anchoring her body against the crystal ceiling, and pulled. 

The argent threads snapped straight. Instead of the Loom pulling life out of her, the silver knot inverted the direction of the flow. The energy surging through the dark ropes violently reversed, shooting backward toward the exterior altar like a severed cable whipping through open air.

***

Outside, the crystal basin cracked.

A sharp, deafening sound like shattering glass echoed through the chamber as a web of fractures spider-webbed across the Lifestone’s surface. 

Nicholas felt the shift instantly. As the internal energy matrix buckled under Elena’s inverted strike, he shoved his arm through the newly formed rift in the crystal wall. Jagged quartz edges sheared into his forearm, slicing flesh to the bone, but his fingers surged forward until they slammed into Elena’s outstretched palm.

The heat of her skin hit him like a furnace blast in the sub-zero tomb.

“Now!” Elena’s voice rang through his mind, stripped of all doubt, pure and blindingly sharp.

Nicholas locked his fingers into hers, using his body as a physical conductor. The argent wave Elena had gathered inside the core surged up her arm, tore through Nicholas’s arm, and erupted from his free palm in a blinding, chartreuse-and-silver flare.

The shockwave hit Rafe-Serpent squarely in the sternum.

The pitch-black shadow-threads binding the ash-husk snapped with the sound of breaking iron cables. Rafe’s chest caved inward. The dry charcoal skin split open, shedding clouds of black dust and burnt bone splinters as the power tore through his reconstituted frame. 

The hollow husk gave a final, rasping shriek before disintegrating completely, collapsing into a heap of grey cinder and smoldering soot on the marble floor.

For half a second, silence filled the vault.

Then something heavy fell out of the collapsing chest cavity of the ash-puppet.

It was an oval relic carved from seamless obsidian, its surface weeping thick, tar-like glyphs that hissed upon contact with the air. The object bounced twice across the stone platform and struck the fractured base of the Loom.

The obsidian did not break. It drank the ambient light instantly.

A deep sound reverberated from beneath the altar floor—not a rumble of earth, but a hollow, subterranean tearing sound. The air above the relic warped, turning violent violet, then pitching into absolute, lightless black.

The marble beneath Nicholas’s boots split wide open.

A localized spatial void tore through the center of the dais, pulling air, shattered crystal fragments, and loose stone into its silent, swirling center. The gravity in the room inverted toward the abyss.

Nicholas slipped. His boots found no traction on the disintegrating marble. Before he could brace his weight against the altar rim, the black tear swallowed his lower half, dragging him down into the lightless throat of the void.

His fingers scraped frantically against the slick quartz floor of the Lifestone, leaving trails of dark blood as the spatial pressure pulled him deeper.

“Nicholas!”

Elena threw herself forward across the crumbling edge of the inner chamber. She did not reach for the stone. She wrapped the glowing silver thread embedded in Nicholas’s chest three times around her bare, bleeding wrists, digging her boots into the lip of the intact crystalline floor.

The pull of the void was absolute, an icy tide threatening to rip her arms from their sockets. 

Nicholas hung over the dark abyss, his face drawn tight with iron resolve, his gaze fixed upward on her as the black spatial rift widened beneath his boots, tugging them both toward the edge of oblivion.
