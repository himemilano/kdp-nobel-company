# Chapter 2

The cold blade of Cassian’s broadsword rested against the hollow of my throat, its edge precise enough to feel the twitch of my pulse. 

"Do not move," he commanded. 

His voice didn't just carry sound; it carried *weight*. The Alpha Command hit me like a physical wave—a sudden, crushing spike of barometric pressure that slammed into my chest and forced my knees deep into the sharp, crushed granite of the crater. 

Without a wolf to buffer the aura, the command hit my human nervous system raw. My ribs creaked. My lungs seized. But where a normal pack member would yield, their inner beast bowing to the apex, my broken bloodline reacted differently. The ancient, geometric threads of the Weaver’s Sight flashed behind my eyelids—a silent, stubborn anchor that refused to let my spirit break.

Around us, the mist churned. Four war-patrol enforcers moved through the smoke in tight formation, their silver-tipped pikes lowered, their eyes glowing faint amber in the pre-dawn gloom. 

"Look at her hands, Alpha," Torin, Cassian’s lieutenant, barked, his pike pointing straight at my chest. His wolf bristled beneath his skin, scenting the air with clear, superstitious dread. "She was touching the raw weave. She’s got gold clinging to her skin. She’s an abomination—a Weaver."

"Silence, Torin," Cassian said. He didn't lift the sword an inch. His amber eyes, dark as burnt honey, narrowed through the drifting ash, boring down into mine. "Wren Halloway. You breached the treaty line twelve minutes ago without logging your crossing. You stood over the fourth ward pillar as it shattered. And now you sit in the ashes of our perimeter with a enemy crest at your boots."

He gestured with his chin toward the jagged block of stone lying near my left foot. The core cylinder, blown clear from the pillar's heart, bore the unmistakable deep-etched mark of a coiled serpent—the Greyford crest.

"Give me one reason," Cassian dropped his voice to a dangerous, low hum that vibrated through the stone beneath us, "not to take your head right here before the pack council asks for it."

I swallowed down the taste of iron and ash. My breath hitched, but I didn't pull back from his steel. 

I bit hard on the inside of my cheek, using the sharp spark of pain to cut through the suffocating haze of his Alpha Command. I wasn't a warrior. I was an unshifted, failed presentation working stone with a hammer. But stone didn't lie, and neither did physics.

I reached out with my bare, bloodied right hand.

"Don't move, girl!" Torin roared, tightening his grip on his pike.

Ignoring him, I snatched up the hot, severed core plug directly from the rubble and thrust it straight up, forcing the jagged rock into the space between my neck and Cassian’s blade. 

Cassian’s eyes flicked down, his focus instantly shifting from my throat to the cylinder.

"Look at it, Alpha," I rasped, my voice steady despite the blood coating my teeth. "Look at the bore holes. You think I blew this pillar up just now? Look at the micro-fractures inside the core channel!"

Cassian’s gaze narrowed. He was a conqueror, but he wasn't a fool. His eyes traced the hairline cracks I pointed to.

"They're oxidized black," I snapped, letting my mason’s knowledge slice through the thick tension like a chisel. "Sulfur-rot doesn't coat a fracture in twelve minutes. It takes at least thirty-six hours of exposure to high-grade alchemical charges. This sabotage was planted *two days ago*—during the joint patrol rotation. When the Greyford envoys were escorted along this exact ridge under your personal peace banner."

A heavy silence fell over the patrol. Torin shifted his boots, his scent spiking with sudden, uneasy realization.

I gestured hard to the tool roll strapped to my thigh. "My roll holds tempered steel chisels and hand scrapers. The bore marks inside this rock were carved with a fluted, military-grade diamond drill bit. A human stone-cutter couldn't hide that kind of heavy hardware in a tool satchel. Your bloody treaty didn't keep the Greyfords out, Cassian. It gave them the drill permits."

Cassian’s blade didn't drop, but the crushing weight of his Alpha aura lightened by a fraction. His mind was working fast, weighing my forensic analysis against the cold reality of the dead stone. "And the gold on your skin?" he asked quietly. "The forbidden magic?"

As if summoned by his words, my vision violently split.

A wave of intense nausea hit me as the Weaver’s Sight roared back to life unbidden. Molten copper seemed to pour straight down my optic nerves. The physical world—the silver pikes, the gray mist, Cassian’s fierce face—was suddenly overlaid by a blinding geometric web. 

I choked back a scream as the sensory strain scorched my brain. Thousands of glowing, thread-like filaments stretched across the mountains, but right beneath our feet, a jagged black thread had been violently stitched into the pack's ward network. 

It wasn't just humming. It was vibrating at a precise, weaponized frequency.

"It's... it's a tuning fork," I gasped, my hands flying to my temples as blood trickled thin and hot from my left nostril. The pain felt like an awl driven into my skull. "The explosion... at Pillar Four... it wasn't a breach."

Cassian leaned in, his broadsword finally lowering an inch as he saw the visceral agony racking my body. "What do you see, Wren?"

"A decoy," I forced out, staring through him at the pulsing black weave that snaked deep underground. "The blast here... was designed to ring out through the root-line. It dragged you and your heavy guard five leagues south to this crater. But the dark thread... it’s feeding the shockwave backward."

I lifted my shaking hand, pointing wildly toward the northeast. "It's radiating straight toward the Eastern Gate. To Ember Hollow."

Torin stepped forward, his eyes wild. "She's lying! She's casting illusions to confuse us! Put the wolfsbane collars on her and drag her to the dungeon!"

"Collar me," I snarled, turning my bloodshot eyes on the lieutenant, "and you can spend the rest of the morning digging Ember Hollow out from under three hundred tons of collapsed mountain! The ward lattice is tearing itself apart in a chain reaction! Only someone who can see the warp and weft can calculate which node is going to blow next and cut the feedback loop!"

Cassian stared at me. For three years, he had seen me as a hollow shell—a pack member who couldn't shift, a quiet stone-cutter who fixed the walls he built. But now, in the gray dawn, he saw the ancient bloodline burning through my skin. 

He didn't offer pity. He didn't offer soft words. He made a ruthless, instant calculation.

"Can you stop the shockwave?" he asked, his voice iron.

"Not from up here," I spat, wiping the blood from my lip. "I have to sever the line where it meets the ground—"

A subterranean groan cut me off. 

The earth beneath the ridge heaved. Deep below the stone, a sound like rending metal echoed up through the rock. Far off to the northeast, three bright violet flares shot into the overcast sky above Ember Hollow—the exact frequency of the corrupted thread.

"They're early," I whispered, the Weaver's Sight blinding me with a surge of dark, violet static.

My eyes snapped toward the treeline barely forty yards down the ridge slope. Through the thick mist, six shapes stepped out from the pine shadows. 

They didn't smell like rogue wolves. They wore cold-forged steel armor designed to mask scent, and their eyes didn't gleam with the gold or silver of a shifter's light. Their gaze reflected an oily, synthetic violet. 

In their hands, the Greyford infiltrators held long, silver-sheathed detonator rods, jammed directly into the exposed root-lines of the mountain bed.

"Contact!" Torin yelled, drawing his broadsword.

Before the enforcers could form a defensive line, Cassian stepped half an inch in front of me. His broadsword cleared its sheath with a lethal, ringing hiss, his massive frame shielding my body from the immediate line of fire.

"Fix the line, Mason," Cassian growled over his shoulder, his voice cold, ruthless, and absolute. "I'll keep them off your back."

I didn't hesitate. I dropped straight onto my stomach, ignoring the agonizing burn behind my eyes, and plunged my bare, bloody fingers straight into the dirt—pinning my gold-lit hands directly onto the live, sparking ward-vein to tear the circuit apart before the detonators clicked.
