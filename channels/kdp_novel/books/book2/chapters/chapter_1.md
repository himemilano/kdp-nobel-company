# Chapter 1

The fourth ward pillar was bleeding. 

Not resin, and not the faint, amber discharge of an overworked ley-stone. Thick, tar-black rot oozed from a jagged puncture wound in the granite, hissing where it dripped onto the frost-crusted pine needles.

I knelt, the copper chisels in my tool roll clinking against the frozen loam. The evening beacon should have chimed twelve minutes ago across the southern ridge, sending its clean, harmonic pulse back to the central keep. Instead, the southern sector of Ember Hollow had gone dead. My supervisor back at the mason’s yard had told me to log the silence and wait for morning patrol. 

I hadn’t logged it. I’d grabbed my field pack, crossed the treaty line into Sector 4, and run straight into the dark.

The air smelled of scorched iron and dead marsh. 

"Stupid," I muttered under my breath, though my hands didn't hesitate. I pulled a broad-headed iron scraper from my belt and jammed the edge against the stone. "You don't just crack. You're five tons of bedrock anchor."

The black sludge gave way with a sickening, wet tear. Beneath the crust, the damage wasn't natural erosion or the shift of an underground fault. The borehole was perfectly circular, bored with a diamond-tipped core drill—military issue. 

Someone had drilled straight through the carved bind-runes that kept the southern wildlands from bleeding into pack territory. 

I leaned closer, my breath pluming white against the dusk. Embedded deep within the tarred drill hole was an obstruction, jammed tight to disrupt the harmonic resonance of the stone. I wedged the chisel beneath it, torquing my weight until the metal shrieked against the stone. 

With a brittle snap, the object dislodged and rolled into my palm.

It was a jagged shard of polished silver. Even caked in caustic sap, the embossed heraldry was unmistakable: a lunging hound framed by two crossed spears. 

The crest of House Greyford. Elite Alpha guard. 

My pulse spiked, hammering in my throat. Greyford had signed the southern peace accord three winters ago. Their patrols had no jurisdiction within two leagues of this marker, let alone permission to core-drill our defensive wards with necrotic rot. This wasn't an accidental breach. It was an act of war, calculated and engineered to drop Ember Hollow’s perimeter without tripping an acoustic alarm.

A sudden tremor rippled through the granite beneath my knees.

The black sap pooled around the broken base didn't sink into the dirt; it began to crawl. It snaked upward, drawn to the heat of my skin. 

Before I could drop the silver shard, a thread of liquid frost shot up my fingers. 

The shock hit my chest like a falling timber. Cold, putrid, and suffocating, the foreign malice invaded my veins, seeking the heart of whatever living thing dared touch it. Most wolves in my pack would have called upon their inner beast to incinerate the taint with sheer predatory heat. 

I didn't have that heat. My wolf had been silent, dormant, and broken since the night of my failed presentation three years ago.

Instead, something else woke up.

Deep behind my sternum, an ancient, calcified lock snapped open. A blinding current of pure, radiant heat roared through my blood, meeting the black rot and obliterating it in an instantaneous burst of white steam.

My vision shattered. 

The dark forest didn't fade—it ripped open. The bark of the pines, the damp moss, the granite pillar—all of it dissolved into a vast, humming loom of glowing lines. Strands of bright, incandescent gold spanned the earth like warp and weft, anchoring the dirt to the sky, weaving the pack’s boundary into a fortress of light. 

*The Weaver’s Sight.* 

The fairy tales told to pack-less orphans whispered of bloodlines from before the first Alpha, bloodlines that didn't bite or tear, but wove the very fabric of the world. Bloodlines that the high courts had executed centuries ago.

I stared at my hands. Golden light bled through the pores of my skin, dancing like live wire along my fingertips. 

Through the golden weave of the forest, I saw the true horror. The Ember Hollow ward wasn't just broken. A massive, pulsing root of pitch-black thread had been spliced directly into the gold, knotting it, twisting the perimeter line backward into an open gate. 

Someone hadn't just vandalized the boundary. They were editing it. Rewriting the territorial geometry to invite a predator inside.

*Crack.*

The granite pillar exploded. 

The kinetic blast blew me backward, my boots skidding across frozen dirt and pine bark. Shards of stone whizzed past my ears, biting into the tree trunks behind me. 

From the treeline where the black thread anchored into the southern dark, the undergrowth tore apart.

A beast lunged into the clearing. It had the silhouette of a border wolf, but its flesh was sunken, its jaw dislocated, dripping the same black pitch that had choked the ward stone. Its eyes were milky cataracts, blind to the physical world, tracking purely on the sudden eruption of my light.

It snapped its jaws, lunging straight for my throat.

I scrambled up, dragging my copper chisel from my belt, bracing to drive it through the creature's eye—

Steel whistled through the freezing air.

A heavy broadsword descended in a brutal, arc-welding stroke. The blade cleaved through the beast’s skull and collarbone in a single, sickening crack, pinning the thrashing carcass to the frozen earth with the force of an executioner's block.

The monster went still. The dark blood hissed against the steel, steaming in the frost.

Four enforcers in matte-black leather hit the clearing, crossbows raised, sweeping the tree line in synchronized, lethal precision. 

And then the owner of the broadsword stepped over the twitching corpse.

Alpha Cassian Vale didn't breathe hard. He wore no armor over his dark woolen tunic, but the aura radiating off him felt heavy enough to buckle the pines. His jaw was set in granite, silver eyes sharp and predatory, taking in the splintered pillar, the military drill marks, and the dead creature pinned beneath his boot.

He hadn't come to rescue me. His war-patrol had been hunting.

Cassian gripped the hilt of his broadsword, yanking it free from the soil with a spray of black grit. He turned. 

The lethal intensity in his gaze didn't stay on the dead trespasser. It traveled across the clearing, slicing through the mist until it slammed directly into me.

I tried to close my fists. I tried to pull my sleeves down. 

It was too late. 

The golden light didn't fade. It coiled around my fingers, pulsing with the rhythmic, impossible cadence of an ancient Weaver’s flame, illuminating the silver crest of Greyford resting in the dirt between my boots.

The enforcers around him froze, their bows twitching, eyes widening in sheer, terrified recognition.

Cassian took a slow, deliberate step toward me. His silver eyes darkened, ruthlessly calm, the heavy authority of his Alpha command pressing down until the air felt too thick to swallow.

"Wren," his voice cut through the dark, cold and unforgiving as a drawn blade. "What did you just break?"
